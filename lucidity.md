# Preserve lucidity

Lucidity is when your work glides. You can see your thoughts and hold on to them, and the software you use doesn't get in the way of whatever you're trying to do.

Most software now causes a loss of lucidification. It's noisy, you spend your time using it, and the work you were trying to do sits there waiting while you figure out how to use it. What you create lives on someone else's servers, behind an account, in a format no other tool can use, so if you leave the product you leave your work there as well.

Therefore, it's our responsibility to remove the fluff until what's left still does the job, without replacing the person using it. You get better by thinking, not by repeating chores, and not by re-explaining yourself to a tool that knows nothing about you. Software is supposed to do the chores for you. It should leave the thinking to you, and it shouldn't make the decisions for you.

Help is fine when you can call on it when needed and send it away when it isn't. Use it to make a draft, tidy something, or repeat an action you already know how to do. Then you can switch back to working on your own. If that isn't possible with the tool you're using, you're stuck.

A boat without a captain doesn't go anywhere. A butler with no household is without purpose. The tools we build extend our abilities, allowing us to operate at a higher level of abstraction.

All your files should be exportable, and the software you use should be open. You should be able to change it, fork it, or take what you made and use something else. You shouldn't be tied to a format that only works in one place or a price that locks you in and makes you feel obliged to use it for the rest of the billing period.

Tools should allow for innovation, not extraction, but the only way this is possible is if we work together to preserve lucidity.
